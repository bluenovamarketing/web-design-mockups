When to Call

Heating Problems Usually Give You Clues.

A furnace or heat pump does not need to fail completely before it deserves attention. Changes in sound, airflow, runtime or temperature can help the technician understand where to begin.

  • The thermostat is calling for heat, but the home is not warming.
  • Some rooms stay colder even after the system runs.
  • The furnace starts and stops more often than expected.
  • Airflow from the registers feels weak or inconsistent.
  • The system makes a new noise or produces an unfamiliar odor.

Heating Decisions

Repair or Replacement Starts With Evidence.

Age alone does not decide the answer. The failure, repair history, equipment condition, comfort performance and scope of the proposed work all matter.

Repair May Make Sense When

  • The problem is isolated and the system is otherwise in serviceable condition.
  • The equipment has not developed a pattern of repeated major repairs.
  • Airflow and comfort concerns can be corrected without replacing the entire system.
  • The repair supports a reasonable plan for continued use.

Replacement May Deserve Review When

  • A major component has failed or repair history is growing.
  • Comfort problems continue after other causes have been addressed.
  • The existing system no longer fits the home's practical needs.
  • The installation requires broader changes that should be planned together.

Does Frost & Kretsch provide emergency heating service?

Frost & Kretsch advertises 24/7 HVAC availability. Call (586) 929-8980 to describe the problem and confirm the current response plan.

Do you service both furnaces and heat pumps?

Yes. The residential heating architecture includes furnace and heat-pump repair, maintenance, installation and replacement information.

What should I check before calling about no heat?

Confirm the thermostat setting, check whether the system has power and note any unusual sounds or recent changes. Do not repeatedly reset equipment or open panels if you are unsure what is safe.

How do I decide between repair and replacement?

The decision should consider the diagnosed problem, repair history, overall equipment condition, home comfort and the scope of work required—not one generic age rule.
