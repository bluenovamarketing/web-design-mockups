<section class=""><div class="wrap"><div class="center"><span class="tag">Where to begin</span><h2>Not sure which one your <span class="gold">child needs?</span></h2></div><div class="prose rv" style="margin-top:44px">
<p class="lead">Some families arrive with a diagnosis and a referral already in hand. Others have only a feeling that something is harder for their child than it should be, and no idea which door to knock on. Both are good reasons to call.</p>
<p>Occupational therapy addresses the physical, sensory and organizational skills a child uses to take part in daily life: handwriting, coordination, sensory tolerance, emotional regulation, dressing, feeding and the stamina to sit through a school day. Speech therapy addresses the ability to be understood and to understand: pronunciation, vocabulary, sentence structure, following directions and holding a conversation.</p>
<p>There is honest overlap between them. Feeding difficulties can be sensory, motor, or both. A child who avoids group work may be struggling with language, or with regulation, or with both at once. If you are unsure, say so when you call. A short conversation usually narrows it down, and the evaluation settles it.</p>
<p>Many children work with one discipline. When a child benefits from both, the therapists coordinate so you are managing one plan and one set of goals rather than two.</p>
