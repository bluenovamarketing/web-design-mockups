In plain language

Speech and language are not the same thing

Knowing the difference helps you understand what your child actually needs, and why two children with similar struggles can need very different support.

Speech is the physical act of producing sound. Lips, tongue and breath working together so the word that comes out is the word your child intended. Language is everything underneath it: finding the right word, ordering words into a sentence, understanding a question, following a two-step direction, knowing how to take a turn in a conversation.

A child can have a large vocabulary and still be hard to understand. Another can speak clearly and still lose the thread of a classroom instruction. We assess both, along with the social side of communication that often matters most to older kids: reading a room, repairing a misunderstanding, joining a group without hovering at the edge of it.

How sessions work

Sessions are built around play and genuine conversation rather than drills at a table. A child who is engaged produces far more language in forty-five minutes than a child who knows they are being tested, and the skills carry over because they were practiced in something close to real life.

For older children and teens, that often means working through things they actually care about: a game, a video, a conversation they found difficult at school last week.

Feeding and oral-motor support

Feeding sits here too, and it surprises many parents. The same muscles that shape speech sounds manage food textures, and picky eating is frequently a sensory or motor issue rather than a behavioral one. If mealtimes are a battle, it is worth asking about.

On waiting

Families are often told to wait and see. Sometimes that is reasonable advice. But waiting has a cost when a child is already aware that they are not being understood, and that awareness shapes how willing they are to keep trying. An evaluation costs you a conversation and gives you a clear answer either way.

Is this right for my child?

You might consider a call if your child…

  • Is hard for others to understand
  • Is late to talk or use sentences
  • Struggles to follow directions or answer questions
  • Has difficulty with back-and-forth conversation
